What is life insurance and how does it work?

Life insurance is a contract with an insurance company. You pay premiums to keep the policy in force, and if you pass away while it's active, the company pays a death benefit to the beneficiaries you name. It's there so that losing you doesn't also mean your family loses the income and the plan.

What's the difference between term and whole life insurance?

Term life covers you for a set period — 10, 20, or 30 years — pays if you pass away during that time, and generally costs less than permanent coverage. Whole life lasts your lifetime as long as premiums are paid, and it builds cash value you can borrow against. Which one fits depends on your actual life — that's the conversation, not a brochure.

What is universal life insurance?

Universal life is permanent coverage with flexibility: you can adjust premiums and the death benefit over time, and the cash value depends on how the policy is built. Indexed universal life links part of that growth to a market index, with limits on both the upside and the downside. Cassidy explains the mechanics before you decide anything.

How much life insurance do I need?

Start with the math: your annual income times the years of it your family would need replaced, plus debts like a mortgage, plus future costs like college — minus coverage and savings you already have. Her coverage calculator gives you that starting estimate in about a minute. The real number is a judgment call, and that part is what she's here for.

What are living benefits?

Living benefits are riders — add-ons to a policy — that let you tap into part of your own death benefit while you're alive, if a serious illness hits. A qualifying chronic, critical, or terminal diagnosis triggers an accelerated payout you can use for treatment, bills, or time off work. Whatever is paid out early reduces what your beneficiaries receive later — that tradeoff is stated up front.

What is an annuity?

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company: you put money in, and later it can pay you income, often for a set number of years or for life. Fixed annuities earn interest at a rate set by the company; fixed indexed annuities link growth in part to a market index, with limits; variable annuities move with investments inside the contract and carry market risk. The types work very differently, so ask for the formulas — and any surrender charges — in writing.

What should I do with an old 401(k)?

When you leave a job, an old 401(k) usually just sits there. A rollover moves that money into another retirement account so it stays part of your plan, and it's one of the things Cassidy helps with. Talk to a tax professional before moving money — she'll tell you the same.

Does life insurance require a medical exam?

Sometimes. Some policies use a short paramedical exam — measurements like height, weight, and blood pressure, plus blood and urine samples, generally under an hour at your home or workplace. Others skip the exam and decide from application answers and database checks; those can be faster, but may cost more or have lower limits. Underwriting is the insurance company's process, and no one can promise the outcome in advance.
